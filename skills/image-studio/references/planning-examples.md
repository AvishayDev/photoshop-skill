# Example plans (adapt; never paste verbatim)

## "Create a photorealistic tiger standing in a dense forest at sunrise"

1. Define the result: photorealistic, 3:2 landscape, tiger as focal point, warm sunrise mood, no text.
2. Check capabilities (generative provider? GIMP?) and search the knowledge base for tiger, forest,
   fur, natural light and compositing notes.
3. Search tiger references showing anatomy and proportions from a side/three-quarter view.
4. Compare more tiger references for stripe variation, fur texture and silhouette; record observations.
5. Research how tiger fur and stripes look under low warm backlight and side light.
6. Research forest references: vegetation layers, trunks, ground cover and depth.
7. Analyze forest light: sunbeams through the canopy, shadows, haze and colour shifts.
8. Choose camera height, composition, tiger scale and pose, and the key light (sun from upper left,
   low elevation).
9. Write the visual specification (palette, textures, depth planes, interaction).
10. Obtain the tiger element (licensed photo or local generative model) and environment elements;
    state limitations if neither is available.
11. Build the forest in separate layers: sky glow, distant haze, mid trees, ground.
12. Add the tiger on its own masked layer, scaled to the trees and ground plane.
13. Refine fur edges where they meet vegetation; occlude paws with foreground grass.
14. Match colour, contrast and light to the sunrise; add rim light from the sun side.
15. Add contact and cast shadows and warm environmental spill.
16. Match sharpness, depth of field and grain; add god rays and atmospheric haze.
17. Render a preview and inspect anatomy, seams, lighting consistency and scale.
18. Fix the detected defects and re-inspect.
19. Save the editable project (.xcf/.ora), export the PNG, verify, keep credits.
20. Report outputs, limitations and attribution; save reusable knowledge.

## "Crop this photo to a square for Instagram" (simple requests still get 10+ steps)

1. Inspect the document's dimensions and resolution.
2. Identify the subject's position and the important content to keep.
3. Decide the target: 1:1, 1080x1080 export.
4. Calculate the largest square crop that keeps the subject well placed.
5. Check the proposed framing for cut-off limbs/text and headroom.
6. Create a checkpoint.
7. Apply the crop.
8. Render a preview and check edges and composition.
9. Verify the canvas is exactly square; export resized to 1080x1080.
10. Verify the exported file and report the paths.
